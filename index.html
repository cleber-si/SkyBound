The built-in limits are a placeholder, not a real telescope limit table — every observatory handles pointing differently. Download the template, enter your altitude/zenith limits per azimuth sector, and load it here. Columns: azimute_from, azimute_to, zenit_angle_max, zenit_angle_security, zenit_angle_free (degrees, zenith angle zero at the zenith); set the azimuth convention above to match your CSV. Sectors left at ZA ≥ 85° are treated as unmeasured — the tool will report “no limit data” there rather than a false limit.

    Comparison fieldSPARC4 · OPD 1.6 m

    Photometric: no polarization optics in the beam. Each star makes one image in each of g, r, i and z.
    Polarimetric: a waveplate (λ/2 or λ/4) and the Savart analyser go into the beam. Each star makes two images, 13.8″ apart at PA 208° (towards the south-west), each with half the flux, and you observe a sequence of waveplate positions.
    For the comparison field, both images must be isolated and inside the edge margin, so fewer stars qualify.     °

    Finds the pointing whose comparison stars are closest in brightness to the target in the chosen band, within the ΔV window below, with Gaia quality, isolation and edge checks on this instrument's detector(s) (s4field basic mode: no exposure model). Drag the field to try another pointing, turn it with Field PA (0° = the FoV's y axis to the north). Edge margin, isolation, a polarimeter's second image and the guide-star range are under Regions….

    Tiles of this instrument's science area cover an area around the target, with the overlap you choose, turned with Field PA. Drag the mosaic on the sky to move it, click a tile (or a cell of the table) to change its status, and copy the table into the observing log. The statuses are kept in this browser, per target and instrument.

    Filters all on is recommended all · none
    How comparison stars are chosen. A star must lie in the ΔV window above (Vcomp − Vtarget, always applied) and pass every filter that is on. The pointing is then the one with the lowest noise penalty η. With no filter on, only the ΔV window and the magnitudes decide.
    FilterRejects a star when…Why
    Gaia RUWERUWE ≥ 1.4 (Gaia's astrometric fit is poor)Often an unresolved binary, a companion closer than ~1″ or a blend. Photometry is usually fine if nothing varies: switch off to use such stars, then check their light curves.
    Gaia VARIABLEGaia flags it as variableA variable comparison puts its variability into the target's light curve.
    Gaia binaryGaia lists it as a non-single star (astrometric, spectroscopic or eclipsing binary)Eclipsing and ellipsoidal binaries vary.
    IsolationA neighbour brighter than its own G + 5 mag lies within 3 × the typical seeing (7.5″ at 2.5″); in polarimetric mode, either imageA neighbour 5 mag fainter adds under 1% to the aperture; a brighter one spoils it, and seeing changes make that vary.
    Search radiusMore than 7′ from the targetKeeps the comparisons close, where transparency and airmass match the target's.
    Edge marginCloser than 12″ to a channel's edge (both images in polarimetric mode). Off: anywhere on the detector. Applies to the target too.Room for the aperture and sky annulus, and for small pointing errors.
    Colour weightNever rejects: weight 1, 0.5 or 0.25 for |Δ(BP−RP)| ≤ 0.3, ≤ 0.5 or more. Off: every star weighs 1.Colour differences leave an airmass trend in the light curve (mostly in g).
    Saturation (Advanced)Its peak exceeds 70% of saturation at the best seeing, with your exposure timesNon-linear or saturated stars ruin the ratio.
    Noise (Advanced)Its noise per exposure exceeds 10 × the target'sBeyond that a star adds under 1% of a target-like weight.
    Readout trails (Advanced)Another star's charge trail during readout adds more than 1% to it (r: columns; i, z: rows; g: both)SPARC4 reads out with the shutter open; the trail depends on the exposure time.
    Basic uses the first seven; the last three need exposure times. Basic's default window (−0.35 to +8 mag) replaces saturation and noise: a star 0.35 mag brighter than a target at 50% of saturation stays under 70%, and the full model has used comparisons up to 8 mag fainter.

    Assumptions
    
    
    
      Limits are yours to supply. SkyBound computes the sky for any site; the pointing limits live entirely in the
      CSV you load. Unmeasured azimuths (ZA ≥ 85°) are reported as “no limit data” rather than a false limit.
    
      Refraction. Site pressure is derived from the selected altitude (≈ 809 hPa at 1864 m); temperature assumed 10 °C.
    
      Observable = pointable + dark. "Dark" uses the Sun-altitude threshold chosen above (default astronomical night, −18°).
    
      Coordinate reduction. Default is precession only (IAU 1976), the mean place of date that mount control systems apply — arcsecond-accurate and shown explicitly (J2000 → of date + offset). Full apparent place (adding nutation and aberration) and "none" are available in the dropdown.
    
      Two timers. Time to limit is the mount's pointing limit alone (comparable to a mount’s own time-to-limit, shown even in daylight); Observable folds in darkness.
    
      Sun & Moon. Sun ≈ 0.01°; Moon from the full Meeus (ELP-2000) series, ≈ 1 arcmin.
    
      Time. Selected observatory's local civil time (fixed UTC offset). The chart runs local noon to noon.
    
      Name lookup. By s4field when it runs; without it, online from the browser (CDS Sesame; a Gaia DR3 ID from GAVO,
      moved to epoch 2000), then the reference tables in js/targets.js (built by tools/build_targets.py; catalogue positions).
      The message under the name says which source answered.
    
      Comparison field. Done by s4field (SPARC4 on the OPD 1.6 m), which runs on your computer: start it with
      python3 s4field/s4field.py --serve from the SkyBound folder. It needs the network only the first time a target is used
      (Gaia DR3 from the ESA archive, or GAVO's reduced copy when ESA is down); cones are then kept in cones/.
      Basic ranks comparison stars by brightness alone (photon noise, within the ΔV window, default 0.35 mag brighter to 8 mag fainter than the target; no readout trails);
      Advanced adds exposure times, seeing, sky, Moon and the airmass at the chosen time. The sky image is a survey of your choice (DSS2 colour by default) drawn from the survey's HiPS tiles (CDS); DSS2 is decades older than Gaia, so
      fast-moving stars sit away from their markers, which show positions at the observing epoch. Some surveys cover only part of the sky (black elsewhere).
